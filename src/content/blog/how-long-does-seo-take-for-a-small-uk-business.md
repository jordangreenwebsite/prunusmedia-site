If you have just launched a website, checking your position on Google every morning can quickly become frustrating. Being discovered by search engines, appearing for suitable searches and gaining enquiries are three different things. They rarely happen together.

Google's [guidance on hiring an SEO](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/do-i-need-seo) gives a broad timeframe of four months to a year to begin seeing potential benefits from SEO work. That is not a guarantee or a minimum waiting period. Your starting point and competition matter.

## What to expect during the first six months

Use these periods as review points rather than ranking deadlines.

| Period | Work to prioritise | Evidence to look for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First month | Technical audit, tracking and priority service pages | Important URLs are accessible and can be indexed |
| Months two and three | Improve local information, service content and internal links | Relevant searches and impressions begin to develop |
| Months four to six | Refine pages using real search and enquiry evidence | Useful clicks, enquiries or clearer evidence of what needs changing |

Some technical changes become visible quite quickly. Others need repeated crawling and more time. The [Google SEO starter guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) explains why it is worth allowing changes time to settle before judging them.

## Why one small business may progress faster than another

An established electrician with genuine reviews, supplier links and a website people already search for starts in a different position from a newly registered business. A website rebuild can even lose visibility if successful older pages are removed without redirects.

Competition also changes the picture. A specialist service in a small town is a very different search from a broad nationwide service. Focus on the jobs you actually want to win, not simply the largest search volumes. Someone searching for roof repairs in a particular town may be much more valuable to a roofer than someone researching roofing materials generally.

Finally, consider the pages themselves. Each important service needs a useful explanation, relevant evidence, an honest description of your coverage and a clear way to enquire. Do not assume that producing twenty almost identical pages is the same as answering twenty different customer questions.

## How to measure progress before the phone starts ringing

Set up [Google Search Console](https://search.google.com/search-console/about) and establish a baseline. Compare similar periods, accounting for seasonal demand.

- **Indexing:** Are the important pages eligible to appear in search?
- **Impressions:** Are relevant searches beginning to show your pages?
- **Clicks:** Are those impressions leading to visits?
- **Enquiries:** Are the visitors asking for work you actually offer?

Position is useful context, but a single average position can hide very different searches and locations. Look at individual queries and landing pages together. If a page is gaining impressions but few clicks, review whether its search title accurately describes what the customer wants. If clicks are increasing but enquiries are not, test the contact form and mobile experience and review whether the service information is clear.

## What about Google Maps and local search?

For businesses that visit customers or serve a particular area, the Google Business Profile deserves attention alongside the website. Keep hours, categories, services and contact details accurate. Request genuine reviews from customers and use photographs that honestly represent the business.

Google describes its local ranking factors in its [Business Profile guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091). Distance is one factor, so a business should not expect to rank identically in every town for every searcher. A useful service area page is not the same thing as inventing an office address.

## When should you change your SEO approach?

If important pages are still excluded from search, investigate the technical cause rather than simply producing more articles. If your website attracts searches for services you no longer offer, update the content and rethink the priorities. If visitors are arriving but cannot contact you easily, fix that before spending more on acquisition.

Ask your SEO provider to show what was changed, why it was changed and what evidence will be used to assess it. No agency can guarantee a particular organic position.

## Make SEO a practical business decision

Work backwards from your most valuable services and the enquiries your business needs. Improve a small number of important pages properly, measure the outcomes and expand where the evidence supports it. That is more useful than promising a fixed ranking by a fixed date.
